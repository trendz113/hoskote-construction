The phone call that started this

Recently, a property owner called with a question that sounded simple: "Sir, I have a BMRDA-approved site. I want to construct a G+3 house. How much will the building approval cost?"

So we asked around. Three different people, three completely different answers — for the exact same site, the exact same G+3 plan.

👷 Contractor Friend #1
Around 20 days processing. Government/municipal fees plus a DD of roughly ₹30,000, depending on the application.
📐 Building Plan Consultant
His professional fee: around ₹25,000. The government challan on top: ₹75,000–₹80,000, depending on calculation. Approval for a BMRDA site typically permits ground floor parking plus two residential floors (G+2), with two kitchens.
👷 Contractor Friend #2
A flat ₹75,000, but the process would take up to 60 days — and this contractor has a track record of delaying everything.

Three numbers. Three timelines. And no way to tell, just from those quotes, how much of each figure is an actual government charge versus someone's professional fee.

This is where most people get stuck. When someone says "building approval will cost ₹80,000," that does not necessarily mean the entire ₹80,000 is their fee. It's usually a mix of government charges and professional charges bundled into one number — and there's no way to negotiate or compare quotes properly until you separate the two.

Calculate the government fee first

Before you accept any quote, get the one number that doesn't depend on who you're talking to: the actual government fee. What's actually inside that "approval fee"

When a number gets quoted to you as one lump sum, it's usually hiding several different components:

ComponentWhat it usually is
Government approval / scrutiny feeCharged by the sanctioning authority to review and approve your plan
Betterment feeA development charge levied by the local planning authority
Security depositRefundable deposit held by the authority during construction
Labour cessA statutory construction labour welfare charge
Architect / Engineer / Consultant feeWhat the professional who prepares and submits your plan charges for their own work
Documentation & application expensesPrinting, submission, and processing costs

The government fee calculator above only calculates the government-side components. The professional fee is separate, and it's worth asking for explicitly rather than accepting one bundled number.

One important point about G+3

A BMRDA-approved site does not automatically mean you can build any number of floors you want. What's actually permitted depends on the applicable planning rules, site dimensions, road width, setbacks, FAR/FSI (floor area ratio), zoning, and other requirements. In the case above, the consultant's own estimate assumed the site would qualify for ground floor parking plus two residential floors (G+2) — not the G+3 the owner originally had in mind. Your architect or engineer, together with the competent authority, needs to confirm what's actually permissible for your specific plot before you plan around a number of floors that may not be approvable.

5 questions to ask before paying anyone

If someone quotes you a single number for "building approval," ask them to break it down. These five questions do that:

  1. What is your professional fee? — separate from anything payable to the government.
  2. How much is the government challan? — this is the part you can verify yourself with the calculator above.
  3. Can you show me the government fee calculation? — a legitimate consultant should be able to walk you through it, not just state a total.
  4. What documents are included in your service? — know exactly what you're paying for.
  5. What is the expected processing time? — so a "60 days, frequently delayed" quote doesn't surprise you later.

Once you have the government fee calculated independently, any quote you receive breaks down into something you can actually evaluate:

Government fees: ₹XX,XXX
Architect / Engineer / Consultant fee: ₹XX,XXX
Other expenses: ₹XX,XXX
Total estimated cost: ₹XX,XXX

That's a quote you can compare against a second or third opinion. A single bundled number isn't.

Does building approval help with a home loan?

A sanctioned building plan is typically an important document in a construction or home loan process — but the exact documents required depend on the bank, the loan type, and your specific property. A sanctioned plan doesn't automatically guarantee loan approval on its own. Ask your bank for its exact document checklist before you start construction, rather than assuming approval alone is sufficient. Quick FAQ

Is the fee calculator official, or a third-party estimate?
It's hosted directly on the Karnataka Government's own bhu-yojane.karnataka.gov.in portal — we link to it directly rather than reproducing our own version, so you always see current, authoritative figures.
Why did three people quote three different amounts for the same approval?
Because each quote bundled different combinations of government fees, professional fees, and expected delays differently — none of them were necessarily wrong, but none were directly comparable without a breakdown.
Does BMRDA approval on my site guarantee I can build G+3?
No — permissible floors depend on planning rules, setbacks, road width, and FAR/FSI for your specific plot, which your architect or engineer needs to confirm with the competent authority.
Does building approval alone guarantee my home loan will be sanctioned?
No — a sanctioned plan is typically an important supporting document, but exact requirements vary by bank and loan type. Confirm your bank's full checklist before starting construction.
