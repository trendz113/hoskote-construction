Encumbrance Certificate, E-Khata & Property Registration in Hoskote — The Complete 2026 Guide

The exact Kaveri 2.0 steps for pulling an EC, why Hoskote uses a different Khata portal than Bengaluru city, and how sale deed registration actually works — written for Hoskote and Bengaluru Rural, not generic Bengaluru-city advice.

Why these three documents, and in what order

If you're buying a plot in Hoskote, building a house, or trying to sell later, these three documents come up constantly — and in that order:

  • Encumbrance Certificate (EC) — proves the property has no registered loans, mortgages, or legal claims. Pull this before you pay any advance.
  • Sale Deed Registration — the legal act that actually transfers ownership. Nothing before this makes you the legal owner, no matter how much money has changed hands.
  • E-Khata — the municipal/panchayat record confirming ownership and tax assessment, usually applied for after registration, using the registered sale deed as supporting proof.

All three now run mostly online through Karnataka's Kaveri 2.0 portal and the connected e-Khata systems — the days of losing a full day at the Sub-Registrar Office are largely gone. But the portal you use for e-Khata depends on where your property is, which trips up a lot of Hoskote buyers who follow Bengaluru-city guides by mistake. More on that below.

Part 1: Encumbrance Certificate (EC)

The EC is a record of every registered transaction — sales, mortgages, gifts, leases — against a property for a period you choose. A "Nil" result (Form 16) means no registered claims were found; if transactions show up, it's a Form 15.

  1. 1Register or log in at kaverionline.karnataka.gov.in (the older kaveri.karnataka.gov.in address now redirects here). New users sign up with a mobile number and email for OTP verification.
  2. 2Start a new application and choose "Encumbrance Certificate — Online EC" from the service menu.
  3. 3Search your property — by Property ID (PID) if you have it, or by district → taluk → hobli/village → survey/site number. You can also search by owner name.
  4. 4Choose your search period. A common window is 13–30 years; for full digital coverage select "After 01/01/2004." Anything older needs a manual request at the Sub-Registrar Office (SRO).
  5. 5Verify the results the portal returns match your property, then proceed to payment.
  6. 6Pay the fee — around ₹10 for the application plus ₹10–₹30 per year searched, via UPI, net banking, or card.
  7. 7Download it. A free, non-certified copy is available instantly. The digitally signed certified copy — the one banks and registrars actually accept — takes 1–3 working days.

Pull a fresh EC right before you pay any advance — not weeks earlier. A clean EC from a month ago doesn't guarantee the property is still clear today.

Part 2: E-Khata — and why Hoskote is different

Read this before you Google "e-Khata Bangalore." Almost every guide online is written for BBMP/GBA limits (Bengaluru city) and points you to bbmpeaasthi.karnataka.gov.in. Hoskote falls under a Gram Panchayat / BMRDA jurisdiction, not BBMP — so Khata records for Hoskote properties go through the Panchayat Raj e-Aasthi portal instead. Using the wrong portal is one of the most common, avoidable delays we see.

If you're not sure which system your plot falls under, the local Gram Panchayat office can confirm it in a couple of minutes.

  1. 1Confirm the right portal for your property's jurisdiction (Panchayat Raj e-Aasthi for most Hoskote plots, not BBMP e-Aasthi).
  2. 2Register and log in using an Aadhaar-linked mobile number, verified by OTP.
  3. 3Search for an existing Khata using your Property ID or owner name to check if a draft or manual Khata already exists.
  4. 4Apply for a new Khata only if none exists. If a manual (paper) Khata already exists, don't apply fresh — that risks creating a duplicate record, which authorities treat seriously. Use the digitisation/transfer option instead.
  5. 5Upload documents — sale deed/title deed, EC, Aadhaar and address proof, latest tax receipts (if any), approved building plan or layout, and property photographs.
  6. 6Pay the service fee — typically ₹40–₹500 depending on whether it's a new Khata or a transfer.
  7. 7Wait for verification. The local Revenue Officer reviews the application and may do a site visit. There's usually a short window to raise objections if the draft Khata has errors.
  8. 8Download the digitally signed e-Khata from the portal once approved — typically 2–15 working days, longer if a site visit is needed.

Part 3: Property / Sale Deed Registration

This is the step that legally transfers ownership. Most of the paperwork now happens online through Kaveri 2.0 — only the final biometric signing needs an in-person visit to the Sub-Registrar Office, and that visit now typically takes 15–20 minutes instead of a full day.

  1. 1Check the guidance value for the property's location on Kaveri 2.0. Stamp duty is calculated on whichever is higher — guidance value or actual sale price — so check this before finalizing the deal.
  2. 2Draft the sale deed with accurate buyer/seller details, full property description (survey number, extent, boundaries), and sale consideration. Get this professionally drafted — errors here cause rejections later.
  3. 3Complete Pre-Registration Data Entry on Kaveri 2.0 — enter party and property details, upload the draft deed, EC, and ID proofs (Aadhaar, PAN) for buyer, seller, and witnesses.
  4. 4Pay stamp duty and registration fee. Stamp duty is 2% (below ₹20L), 3% (₹20–45L), or 5% (above ₹45L) of the higher of sale price/guidance value, plus applicable surcharge and cess — plus a further 2% registration fee. Pay via e-Stamping on the portal.
  5. 5Book an SRO appointment. Select the jurisdictional Sub-Registrar Office — for Hoskote-area property, that's the Hoskote SRO — and pick a slot. During peak season (Oct–Mar) slots can be booked out 2–3 weeks in advance.
  6. 6Visit the SRO in person. Buyer, seller, and two witnesses attend with original Aadhaar and PAN for biometric verification and signing — the data entry is already done, so this part is fast.
  7. 7Download the registered deed. The digitally signed sale deed and receipt are generated after execution — this is what makes the ownership transfer legally complete.

Common mistakes that cause delays

  • Using the BBMP e-Khata portal for a Hoskote property. Wrong jurisdiction, wrong portal — wastes time and can generate confusing rejection messages.
  • Applying for a fresh Khata when a manual one already exists. This risks creating a duplicate property record instead of simply digitising the existing one.
  • Registering at a value below the guidance value to save on stamp duty. This is treated as tax evasion and can trigger scrutiny well after the transaction.
  • Booking an SRO slot without all documents ready. A missing document at the appointment usually means a reschedule — and slots during peak months (Oct–Mar) can be 2–3 weeks out.
  • Pulling an EC too early. An EC from a month before your advance payment doesn't guarantee the property is still clear — pull it right before you commit money.

Quick FAQ

Do I need an EC if I'm not taking a loan?
Yes. Sub-Registrar offices require a clean EC before registering a sale deed regardless of whether a loan is involved, and it's the main proof the property is free of registered claims.
Is e-Khata the same thing as ownership proof?
Not exactly — it's a municipal/panchayat tax and record document, not a title deed. The registered sale deed is the legal ownership document; the e-Khata reflects that ownership in local revenue records for tax purposes.
My property already has a manual Khata — do I still need to apply?
You need to digitise/transfer the existing Khata into the e-Khata system, not apply for a brand-new one. Applying fresh when a manual record exists can create a duplicate entry, which authorities treat as a serious issue.
How long does the whole process take, start to finish?
EC: a few minutes to 3 working days for the certified copy. Registration: online prep can be done in a day, then an SRO slot (sometimes 1–3 weeks out in peak season) for the final signing. E-Khata: typically 2–15 working days after registration, longer if a site visit is required.
Can I do all of this myself, or do I need an agent?
All three processes are designed to be done directly by the property owner online — no middleman is required, and official guidance explicitly discourages using agents for e-Khata or EC. That said, for high-value transactions it's worth having a lawyer review the sale deed and title chain.
