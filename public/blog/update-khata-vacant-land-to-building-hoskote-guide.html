How the gap happens

When you buy a plot and get your building approved, your khata and tax record describe a vacant site. On day one, that is correct. Then the house goes up, and the record does not change by itself. The panchayat or municipality has no way of knowing your slab was cast last year unless the change is reported, inspected or applied for.

It matters because the law treats vacant land and buildings as different things. Karnataka's municipal law defines vacant land as land that is not built upon, and it lets local bodies tax buildings, vacant land or both. Tax on a building generally depends on its built-up area, how it is used, its zone and its age. Panchayat property tax follows the same logic: type of property, use and built-up area. A record that says “vacant” is under-describing your property, and the difference between the two is a tax difference.

Your BMRDA layout approval does not fix this. Approval covers the layout and your plan sanction. Whether the khata and tax record show a building is a separate step with the Town Municipal Council or Gram Panchayat that keeps your record.

A composite example (not a real client)

Suresh buys a 30×40 site near Hoskote, takes plan approval, builds a G+1 house and moves in. The property tax, when it arrives, comes at the vacant-site rate, and he pays it dutifully. Four years later he is transferred to Pune, and a buyer loves the house. The buyer's bank pulls the khata extract, which says vacant site. The bank's valuer has just photographed a two-storey house. The documents disagree, the loan stalls, and the buyer's lawyer asks Suresh to correct the record first.

Now Suresh has to fix it while a buyer waits, and the office works out what four years of difference looks like. A short application at completion has become a deal-threatening delay.

60-second self-check

Answer three questions. This is a quick guide, not a legal verdict.

1. Is your house (or new floor) complete and in use?
2. What does your latest khata extract or tax receipt say about the property?
3. Have you applied to update it since the house was completed?

Five moments when “vacant” comes back to bite you

1. When you sell

A buyer's lawyer and bank read your khata extract, then look at the house. If the two disagree, expect to be told to fix the record before any money moves.

2. When you take a loan

Home loan top-ups and loans against property involve a valuation report. Lenders compare it with your records, and a mismatch slows everything down.

3. When the property passes to someone else

Inheritance, partition and khata transfer all start from what is on record. An error at the start gets copied into every later document.

4. When you need utility connections or completion papers

After a Supreme Court direction linking basic services to occupancy certificates, the state has relaxed the OC requirement for small houses, with conditions on plot size and floors that have been changing. Being exempt from an OC is not the same as having a correct khata. Ask your office which completion document it wants from you.

5. When someone official checks

The 2024 changes to Karnataka's municipal law added fines for municipal officers who fail to collect property tax dues, which gives them every reason to look harder at under-declared properties. In Bengaluru, BBMP has already compared sale deeds against declared property details and sent show-cause notices to tens of thousands of owners. Hoskote is not BBMP, but the direction is the same: records are being checked against reality.

What waiting really costs

If the record still says vacantIf you update it now
Tax The authority can work out the shortfall between vacant-site tax and building tax for the period the house has existed. Every extra year adds to it. You pay building tax going forward, and the shortfall covers only the short gap since completion.
Penalty Overdue amounts can attract interest or penalty under your local body's rules. The rate varies, so ask your office. Coming forward on your own is usually an easier conversation than being caught out.
Sale or loan Delayed or stalled while you correct records under pressure. Clean, matching documents from day one.
Khata services Unpaid tax can block khata issuance or renewal, and with it further approvals. Tax current, records consistent, no surprises.

If your house is bigger than the approved plan (extra floor, extra area), do not try to hide it in the update. Get it measured and disclosed, and ask your architect or engineer about regularising it. A deviation you declare is a problem you can work through. One an officer discovers is far worse. Karnataka has also proposed charging double tax in the first year on buildings that violate bylaws, so check the current position with your local office.

How to update it, in order

First, find out who keeps your record. Inside Hoskote town limits, that is normally the Town Municipal Council. Most plots outside the town sit under a Gram Panchayat, where records are on the Panchayat Raj e-Aasthi / e-Swathu system (Form 9/11). If you are not sure, the local office can tell you in a couple of minutes.

  1. Gather your paper trail. Approved plan, sale deed, EC, latest tax receipt, khata extract, and any commencement or completion papers.
  2. Settle the completion document. Ask the office whether it wants an occupancy certificate, a completion certificate, or a small-house exemption note. Do not assume.
  3. Get the as-built area measured. A short certificate from your architect or engineer, showing built-up area by floor, and how it compares with the sanctioned plan.
  4. Get your permanent electricity connection if you still have a temporary one, and keep the bill. It is useful proof the building exists and is in use.
  5. Check what your record says today. Download your current khata extract (or Form 9/11) and read the property type and built-up area.
  6. Apply for the update. Submit the application to the Town Municipal Council or Gram Panchayat, with the documents above and photographs of the completed house. Offices name this step differently (revised assessment, building details update, khata correction), so use their wording.
  7. Pay what is assessed, and ask for it in writing. Ask for the demand on the portal or on paper, with the calculation, so you know exactly what you are paying for.
  8. Collect and file the proof. Get the updated extract and a fresh tax receipt. Keep two paper copies and one scanned copy. You will need them the next time anyone asks.

The excuses that cost people money

What people sayWhat actually happens
“Nobody has asked me.” Nobody asks until a sale, a loan or an inspection. At that point it is urgent, and you pay under pressure.
“I'm not selling.” A loan, a partition or an inheritance will still need the record. Fix it while you have every document in one place.
“It's only a small house.” The record does not care about size. Small houses are exactly where mismatches sit unnoticed for years.
“My contractor will handle it.” Most contracts end at handover, and the record belongs to the owner. Read yours, and ask in writing before the final payment.
“I'll deal with it later.” The gap between the record and the house only gets wider with time, and the amount assessed usually grows with it.

The rule of thumb: the day your house is complete is the day you start the update. Put it on the handover checklist next to the final payment and the Vaastu puja.

Quick FAQ

My plot is in a BMRDA-approved layout. Does this still apply to me?
Yes. Layout approval covers the layout and your plan sanction. Your khata and tax record are kept by the local body, and they need to show that a building now stands on the plot.
I pay property tax every year. Doesn't that mean I'm fine?
Not necessarily. You may have been paying at the vacant-site rate. Look at your latest khata extract and tax receipt and check whether they show a building and its built-up area.
Do I need an occupancy certificate first?
It depends. Karnataka has relaxed OC requirements for small residential houses, with conditions on plot size and number of floors, and the rules have been changing. Ask your Town Municipal Council or Gram Panchayat which completion document it will accept for your house.
My house is larger than the sanctioned plan. What do I do?
Get it measured, speak to your architect or engineer about regularisation, and disclose the actual built-up area. Hiding a deviation makes the eventual problem bigger.
How long does the update take?
It varies by office and by how complete your papers are. Having the approved plan, as-built measurement and tax receipts ready is the biggest thing you control.
What happens if I do nothing?
Often nothing visible, until a sale, a loan or an inspection. Then you face the tax shortfall, any penalty or interest your local body applies, and a delay in whatever you were trying to do.
