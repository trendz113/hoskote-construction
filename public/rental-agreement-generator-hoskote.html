Rental Agreement in India: The Complete Guide for Owners & Tenants (2026)

Everything you need to know before you rent out or rent a house — in plain language, no legal jargon.

Why a written rental agreement matters

A verbal understanding might work fine for months — until it doesn't. A written rental agreement protects both sides: it fixes the rent so it can't be changed on a whim, records the deposit amount so it isn't "forgotten" at move-out, and gives the owner a documented notice period and remedy if the tenant overstays. It also serves as an address proof document for the tenant and a rent-receipt reference for the owner's income tax filing.

Rental Agreement vs Lease Deed vs Leave & License

In everyday use these terms are used loosely, but there are differences. A lease transfers a right to exclusive possession for a fixed term and is technically governed by the Transfer of Property Act; long-term leases must be registered. A leave and license agreement (common in Maharashtra) only grants permission to occupy without transferring possession rights, making it easier to terminate. Most residential arrangements in Karnataka, including Hoskote, use a simple rental/lease agreement format renewed every 11 months, which is what our free generator above produces.

Why almost every agreement in India is for "11 months"

Under the Registration Act, 1908, any lease for 12 months or more must be compulsorily registered at the Sub-Registrar's office, which involves higher stamp duty and registration fees, plus both parties' physical presence. Keeping the term at 11 months avoids this requirement, so the agreement can be executed on plain stamp paper and notarised instead — cheaper and faster. It is simply renewed (often with a fresh document) every 11 months for as long as both sides are happy to continue.

Information you must collect before drafting the agreement

  • Owner details: full legal name, permanent address, ID proof number (Aadhaar/PAN), and proof of ownership (khata/sale deed) for the property.
  • Tenant details: full legal name, permanent/native address, ID proof number, current employer or reason for stay, and number of occupants.
  • Property details: exact address, type (independent house, portion, flat, commercial), built-up area, and a list of fittings/furniture provided.
  • Commercial terms: monthly rent, security deposit, rent due date, mode of payment, and whether maintenance/water/BESCOM bills are included.
  • Term & exit terms: start date, duration, lock-in period (if any), notice period for either party, and renewal terms.
  • Consequence clauses: late-payment penalty, holdover/non-vacation penalty, and deposit deduction conditions for damage.
  • Witnesses: two witnesses with names, addresses, and signatures at the time of signing.

What actually happens if a tenant doesn't vacate on time

This is the concern we hear most often from Hoskote homeowners. In practice, the process is: first, the owner sends a formal written notice (WhatsApp plus a physical/registered letter) reminding the tenant of the vacate date already agreed in the contract. If the tenant still doesn't leave, the holdover clause in the agreement kicks in — commonly, one month's rent (or the penalty you selected in the generator) is forfeited from the security deposit, and rent for the extra period is charged at a higher, pre-agreed rate. If the tenant continues to refuse to vacate beyond this, the owner's remedy is a legal eviction suit before the local civil court or rent authority — physical "self-help" eviction (changing locks, cutting power, forcibly removing belongings) is not legal and can backfire on the owner. This is exactly why having the clause in writing, agreed and signed at the start of the tenancy, matters — it gives the owner a documented, enforceable basis to act instead of relying on goodwill after the relationship has soured.

Security deposit — how much and how it's protected

There's no single legal cap on deposits in Karnataka today, though the Model Tenancy Act (adopted by some states) recommends up to two months' rent for residential lets. Around Bengaluru it's common for owners to ask for anywhere from 5 to 10 months' rent as deposit, particularly for independent houses. Whatever figure you agree, write it clearly into the agreement along with the refund timeline (commonly 30 days after vacating, post adjustment of dues) so there's no ambiguity at move-out.

Stamp duty & registration — do you need to register it?

For agreements up to 11 months, most owners in Karnataka execute the document on non-judicial stamp paper of an appropriate value and get it notarised, rather than registering it. Stamp duty amounts vary by state and are periodically revised, so confirm the current value with your local stamp vendor or sub-registrar's office before finalising. If you want the strongest legal protection — for high rent values, long terms, or complex tenancies — registration at the Sub-Registrar's office is the safer route.

Tips for owners renting out property in Hoskote

  • Verify the tenant's ID and take a copy of Aadhaar/PAN before handing over keys — many RWAs and police stations also ask for a "tenant verification" form to be filed locally.
  • Photograph the property's condition (walls, fittings, meter readings) before move-in and attach it as an annexure to the agreement.
  • Collect post-dated cheques or set up a UPI standing instruction for rent to avoid monthly follow-ups.
  • Never accept rent in a way that leaves no paper trail — issue simple rent receipts every month.
  • Keep the holdover/non-vacation clause specific and reasonable, so it's actually enforceable and not seen as one-sided if challenged.

Tips for tenants

  • Read the deposit refund clause carefully — check what deductions are allowed (damage, unpaid bills) versus normal wear and tear, which should not be deducted.
  • Confirm in writing what's included in rent (water, maintenance) so there are no surprise monthly add-ons.
  • Keep your own copy of the signed agreement and all rent payment proofs — you'll need these for HRA tax exemption claims too.
  • Give notice in writing (not just verbally) when you plan to vacate, and get an acknowledgement.

Rental agreement FAQs

Short answers to what Hoskote landlords and tenants ask us most. This is general information, not legal advice.

📄 Rental & Lease Agreements
Q1 · Is a written rental agreement compulsory in Karnataka?▾

It's not compulsory for every tenancy, but it is strongly advisable. Without a written agreement, disputes over rent, deposit refund, or vacating dates come down to one person's word against another's. A signed document — even a simple 11-month one on stamp paper — gives both the owner and tenant enforceable proof of what was agreed.

Q2 · Why do rental agreements always say "11 months"?▾

Agreements for 12 months or longer must be compulsorily registered at the Sub-Registrar's office under the Registration Act, 1908, which costs more and takes more time. Keeping the term at 11 months lets the agreement be executed on stamp paper and notarised instead, then renewed every 11 months by mutual consent.

Q3 · If a tenant doesn't vacate on time, can I deduct their deposit?▾

Yes, if your agreement has a holdover/non-vacation clause specifying this (our free generator above includes one by default). You can deduct the agreed penalty — commonly one month's rent — from the security deposit and charge a higher holding-over rent for the extra period. For a tenant who continues to refuse to leave, the next step is a formal legal notice followed by an eviction suit; forcibly removing a tenant yourself is not legal.

Q4 · How much security deposit can I ask a tenant for?▾

There's no fixed legal cap currently in Karnataka. Around Bengaluru and Hoskote, owners commonly ask for 5–10 months' rent as deposit for independent houses, though this is negotiable. Whatever you agree, state it clearly in the agreement along with when and how it will be refunded.

Q5 · Is this free rental agreement generator legally valid?▾

The generator produces a standard, commonly used template covering the essential clauses. For it to be legally executed, print it on the correct value non-judicial stamp paper for your state, have both parties and two witnesses sign it, and get it notarised (or registered, if the term is 12 months or more). For high-value or disputed tenancies, we recommend a quick review by a local advocate before signing.

Q6 · What documents should I collect from a tenant before renting out my house?▾

At minimum: a copy of Aadhaar or PAN, a passport-size photo, current employer or business details, and a local police "tenant verification" form if your area requires one. Keep a copy of the signed rental agreement and photograph the property's condition before handing over keys.

Q7 · Can I increase the rent during the agreement period?▾

Generally no — not unless the agreement itself has a clause allowing a revision (for example, an annual increase on renewal). The rent fixed in a signed agreement is binding for its term. Any increase should be agreed in writing, ideally at the time of renewing the 11-month term.

Q8 · Do I need to pay tax on rental income?▾

Yes, rental income is taxable under "Income from House Property" in your income tax return, after a standard 30% deduction for repairs/maintenance and deduction of home loan interest if applicable. Keeping a signed rental agreement and rent receipts makes this straightforward to report and also helps your tenant claim HRA exemption if they're salaried.
